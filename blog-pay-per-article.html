Pay Per Article, Not Per Hour

The payment model that made her business work — and why hourly pay would have failed.

Rashmi's writers are paid per article, not per hour or by salary. That one decision kept her business alive at the start, helped it scale mid-stage, and aligned every writer with output. Paying by time sounds fair — but at her stage it would have created costs she couldn't afford and speed she never got.

What She Actually Does

  • Writers are paid per article based on how many articles they write.
  • No fixed salary — income scales directly with output.
  • High-output writers (those who need money most) do 60–70 articles a day and earn ₹20,000–30,000/month.
  • Her total team cost is ~₹1.5 lakh/month — and it grows only when income grows.

Why Pay-Per-Article Helped Her Succeed

Costs only rise when income rises

  • At the start, income was low — a salary bill would have eaten all profit.
  • Per-article pay means she pays for output she can actually sell.
  • Her risk stays low at every stage: no fixed payroll, no cash burn.

It creates speed and volume

  • Writers are motivated to write more — more articles = more money for them and for her.
  • This is how 25–30 posts/day on her blog became possible.
  • Aligned incentives: the writer's goal (more output) is exactly her goal.

It rewards the right writers

  • Writers who need money most work hardest and earn most — ₹20k–30k/month.
  • Lazy or slow writers simply earn less; no one carries dead weight.
  • Quality still matters — articles must meet the standard to count.

Why Hourly or Salary Pay Would Not Have Succeeded

At the start

  • Income was low and irregular — a fixed payroll would have ended the business.
  • Untrained friends were slow; paying per hour for slow learners is a fast loss.
  • Per-article let her train people while paying only for usable results.

At the mid-stage

  • Scaling from a few writers to 15 needs costs that scale with revenue.
  • Hourly pay rewards time, not output — writers have no reason to be fast.
  • Salary creates fixed costs even in bad months (traffic crashes, penalties, AdSense disabled).

The incentive trap

  • Paid by the hour → work expands to fill the hours.
  • Paid per article → speed and volume are rewarded directly.
  • Her entire volume system depends on the second incentive.

How to Apply This Model

  • Pay per deliverable (article, task, project), not per hour.
  • Set a clear per-article rate and a quality standard that must be met.
  • Only add team members when output can cover their pay.
  • Reward speed and volume publicly — your best writers become your engine.
  • Keep fixed costs near zero until income is stable and predictable.
