20–21 AdSense Rejections — Then Approval

She was rejected 20–21 times. She kept applying and improving — never stopped.

Rashmi's AdSense application was rejected 20–21 times before she finally got approval. Most people quit after two or three. She treated every rejection as a to-do: improve the site, publish more, reapply. This is how you keep going even when the system keeps saying no.

What Actually Happened

  • She applied for AdSense once traffic started to appear.
  • AdSense rejected her — again and again, 20–21 times.
  • She didn't stop applying. She also didn't stop improving the site.
  • She kept publishing daily throughout the rejections.
  • Finally, 3–4 months into the Hindi blog, approval came.
  • Around November, Google Discover switched on — and ran continuously after that.

Why She Kept Going

Rejection is a report card, not a verdict

  • Each rejection told her the site still wasn't ready — content, traffic, or policy.
  • Instead of taking it personally, she read it as "keep building."
  • She improved the website and continued publishing between attempts.

Apply again is a strategy, not stubbornness

  • AdSense requirements are about content quality and traffic — both grow over time.
  • Reapplying after meaningful improvement is the correct process.
  • Approval came when the site finally met the bar — not because she got lucky.

Track every rejection

  • Keep a simple log: date applied, date rejected, reason, and what you fixed.
  • Only reapply after fixing something real — new content, better structure, more traffic.
  • Tracking turns "I failed 21 times" into "I improved 21 times."

The Rejection Tracker Template

  • Attempt # — 1, 2, 3… 21.
  • Date applied and date rejected.
  • Reason given (thin content, low traffic, policy, etc.).
  • What I fixed before reapplying.
  • Traffic at that time — watch it climb each attempt.
