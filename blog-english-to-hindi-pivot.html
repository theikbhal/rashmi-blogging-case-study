Failure to Pivot: From English Blog to Hindi Blog

Her first website flopped. The second one found her first success — after 6 months.

Rashmi's first website was in English. It got almost no traffic for 4–5 months. Instead of quitting blogging, she pivoted — changed the language, restarted, and got her first real response about 6 months later. Here's exactly how the pivot worked.

What Actually Happened

  • She created her first website in English — she didn't speak or write English confidently at the time.
  • She ran it for 4–5 months with almost no reach or traffic.
  • She accepted the failure, analyzed why, and did not quit blogging.
  • She created a second website in Hindi, applied the lessons, and kept publishing.
  • About 6 months later, the Hindi site started getting a real response.

Why the Pivot Worked

She changed the market, not the skill

  • The skill (WordPress, SEO, publishing, RankMath) stayed the same — only the language changed.
  • Hindi content had far less competition and a huge underserved audience.
  • She could write naturally and fast in her mother tongue — speed and volume became possible.

She learned in her own language too

  • Her first YouTube mentor videos were in English — she skipped them.
  • The second video was in Hindi (Satish Kushwaha) — that's when she understood how to set up SEO and RankMath.
  • Understanding the why in your own language builds real confidence (see Learn in Your Mother Tongue).

Success took time — 6 months, not 6 weeks

  • AdSense approval came only after 20–21 rejections.
  • Google Discover went live around November of that year, then ran continuously.
  • The lesson: the second attempt works if you stay consistent through the silent months.

The Pivot Playbook (Copy This)

  • Give your first attempt a fixed trial window (4–5 months), then evaluate honestly.
  • Analyze the failure: was it the content, the language, the niche, or the consistency?
  • Pivot the weakest variable — often the language or niche — not the whole skill.
  • Expect ~6 months of quiet before Google responds. Keep publishing daily.
  • Treat rejections as feedback and reapply; don't stop improving the site.
