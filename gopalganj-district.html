District Profile · Bihar · 2011 Census

Gopalganj District

2.56 million people · 1,566 villages · home of Rashmi Gupta

Gopalganj is a district in the Saran division of Bihar, India. Rashmi Gupta — the 21-year-old student earning ₹3 lakh/month from news blogging — belongs to this district. The district-level figure (population 25.62 lakh) is the correct context for her story, since she says she is from Gopalganj district, not specifically Gopalganj town.

Why the District Figure Matters Here

District vs town

  • In her interview, Rashmi says she "belongs to Gopalganj, which comes in Bihar" — the district, not just the town.
  • Population 2,562,012 (25.62 lakh) is the district total from the 2011 Census — the right number to cite.
  • She moved from the district to Gorakhpur for her B.A. in Psychology (final year), commuting between study and business.

How her location shaped her success

  • Low cost of living: a small-town budget meant she could reinvest nearly everything she earned.
  • Hindi-language market: from a Hindi-speaking district, she pivoted to Hindi content after her English site failed.
  • Local team: her 15-person team started with roommates and friends in Gorakhpur — people she met after leaving Gopalganj.
  • News hunger: districts like Gopalganj have real demand for local/regional news, a gap she fills with a 2-state news operation (Gorakhpur + Gopalganj).

Connection to the 100-point roadmap

  • Place advantage (Phase 1): being in a Hindi-speaking region made the Hindi pivot (Phase 3) natural and profitable.
  • People (Phase 8): roommates and friends-turned-writers all came from her local student network.
  • Regional expansion (Phase 10): she registered her news media operation in two states — Gorakhpur and Gopalganj.

"What if" — the location angle

  • Big metro city: higher costs and more distractions, but also more opportunities and tools.
  • No local network: without roommates and friends to train, building a 15-person team at low cost would be much harder.
  • Non-Hindi region: the pivot that saved her first failed English site may not have been as easy elsewhere.
