The 4–5 Writers Who Needed Money Most

No father. Real financial need. They became her most loyal, hardest-working team.

Among her 15 writers, 5–6 had a financial need that was urgent — several had no father. Rashmi didn't just hire them. She spoke to their mothers and guardians, convinced them to let their children work, took personal responsibility, and trained them properly. Today those writers earn ₹20,000–30,000 a month. This may be the most underrated part of her success.

What Actually Happened

  • 5–6 of her writers really needed money — some had no father at all.
  • Instead of treating them as cheap labor, she spoke to their mothers and family members.
  • She convinced the guardians: "I take responsibility. Trust me — I will teach these kids properly."
  • She trained them properly, step by step, like every other writer.
  • Today those same writers earn ₹20,000–30,000 per month.

Why This Was Critical to Her Success

Necessity is the best motivator

  • Writers who genuinely need money work harder than writers who want pocket change.
  • Several of her highest-output writers produce 60–70 articles a day — morning to night.
  • Their need became her most reliable production engine.

Earning changed their families' lives — and their loyalty

  • By convincing guardians and taking responsibility, she gave these families a real income.
  • That changed how her writers saw her: not a boss, but someone who changed their lives.
  • Loyal, motivated writers with a personal stake are almost impossible to poach.

Trust was the real hiring filter

  • She didn't filter by resume — she filtered by need, trust, and willingness to learn.
  • Guardian approval meant the family was invested in the writer's consistency.
  • Taking responsibility (saying "I'll teach them properly") made her accountable to their growth.

How to Copy This Mindset

  • Hire people who genuinely need and value the work — not just anyone who applies.
  • If a young writer needs family permission, talk to the family directly.
  • Take responsibility for your people's success — training is a promise, not an option.
  • Pay fairly per article so hard work is directly rewarded (₹20k–30k/month is proof).
  • Understand that motivation, loyalty, and urgency often beat experience.
