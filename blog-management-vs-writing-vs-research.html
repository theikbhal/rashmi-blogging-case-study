Management vs Writing vs Topic Research

"Management is very hard. Content writing is the easiest. Research is easy too — it depends how you learn it."

In one answer, Rashmi ranked the three jobs in her business: writing is easiest, topic research is easy once learned, and management is the hardest. Understanding why she ranked them this way tells you exactly where your effort should go.

How She Ranked the Three Jobs

  • Content writing — easiest. Once you learn the format, writing is just executing the process.
  • Topic research — easy, but depends on you. It's a learnable skill; her multiple-phones system makes it simple.
  • Management — hardest. Coordinating 15 writers, 13 websites, quality, and deadlines is the real work.

Breaking Down Each Role

Content writing — the easiest to learn

  • Follow the repeatable format: topic → draft → AI polish → verify → publish.
  • Writers who learn the process can do 25–30 posts/day.
  • Skill is real, but it's mechanical once mastered — that's why it's "easiest."

Topic research — easy with a system

  • Watch Discover on multiple phones; write what keeps repeating.
  • Verify facts from official websites.
  • Without a system it feels hard; with a system it's a daily habit.

Management — the hardest, and the real bottleneck

  • Coordinating a 15-person team across 13 websites.
  • Assigning work, monitoring quality, paying per article, keeping deadlines.
  • People, not content, are the hard part — this is what limits growth.
  • She separated management income from content income because management is a distinct, valuable job.

What to Learn From This Ranking

  • Start by learning writing and research — they're the easiest and the foundation.
  • Hire writers to handle writing and research early; don't hoard the easy tasks.
  • Invest your own energy in management — systems, people, quality control.
  • Charge separately for management work when you manage others' websites.
  • The harder the job (management), the more valuable it becomes as you scale.
